AI Memory Handbook ​

From Context Memory to Cognitive Infrastructure: A Full-Lifecycle Practice Guide for Enterprise AI Memory Systems

Version 2026.11 · Data and facts snapshot as of 30 October 2026 Author: Hu Jingkun (胡景堃) · Licensed under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0

中文版 · Full contents (Chinese) · Reading guide (Chinese)

This is the complete English edition — all 23 chapters and 2 appendices, translated in full, not abridged.

Part I · Architecture (Chapters 1–3) ​

Memory is not an accessory feature. It is simultaneously the input to context, the state of the runtime, and the evidence of governance — and those three identities have incompatible correctness requirements. Merging them into "a piece of text we stored" is the common root cause of most memory incidents in production.

  • Ch. 1 — Why Memory Became a First-Class Primitive — Four failure modes, the three identities of memory, three fundamental tensions, and the honest boundary of what a memory system can and cannot do in 2026.
  • Ch. 2 — A Reference Architecture for Memory Systems — Five responsibility domains with an explicit guarantee strength for each. Confusing best-effort with strong-guarantee is how audit and deletion end up permanently unimplemented.
  • Ch. 3 — Form and Maturity: L1–L4 — Graded on responsibility and verifiability rather than autonomy. Each level jump has a different cost character: discipline, then verification, then organization.

Part II · Build (Chapters 4–7) ​

  • Ch. 4 — Writing: Extraction, Typing, Conflict Resolution — Single-pass ADD-only is a choice about the shape of failure, not about speed. Dual time axis, six conflict kinds, four actions.
  • Ch. 5 — Storage: Tiering, Scoping, Lossless Compression — Isolation correctness guaranteed by structure rather than discipline; hot/warm/cold tiering as reversible demotion; AIC compression that restores byte-for-byte.
  • Ch. 6 — Retrieval: Multi-Signal Fusion and Budget Control — Why pure vector retrieval fails on long-horizon memory, why budget must participate in selection, and how to diagnose ranking versus budget errors.
  • Ch. 7 — Integration: MCP, API, and Framework Adapters — Designing a 55-entry tool surface (44 native + 11 client-compatible, 3 deprecated aliases, 52 distinct capabilities); degradation must be reported, never silent.

Part III · Run (Chapters 8–11) ​

  • Ch. 8 — Deployment: What Local-First Really Means — Zero dependencies as an architectural outcome: four consequences and their costs, four deployment forms, the self-host versus SaaS boundary.
  • Ch. 9 — State Recovery, Asynchrony, and Consistency — Sync versus async writes, the flush barrier as the only way out of read-after-write inconsistency, events, idempotency, and recovery drills.
  • Ch. 10 — Scoping for Multi-User and Multi-Agent — Physical isolation turns a cross-tenant leak into a missing file; the namespace derivation trap passes every test and still leaks; cross-agent sharing remains unsolved.
  • Ch. 11 — Operating at Scale: Cost, Latency, Capacity — Four cost structures, why the SQLite ceiling is set by write concurrency rather than data volume, and the metrics that reveal recall degrading over session length.

Part IV · Governance (Chapters 12–15) ​

  • Ch. 12 — Auditability: Hash Chains, Provenance, Evidence — A log is the system's statement about itself; an audit is material for independent review. The hash chain's real value is first_broken_at.
  • Ch. 13 — The Four Semantics of Deletion and Forgetting — Mark-delete, mask, cascade soft-delete, physical delete. Deletion verifiability remains unsolved, and this book does not claim otherwise.
  • Ch. 14 — Pre-Write Safety: Notary, Injection, Credentials — Safety must happen before the write. Three classes of attack need three layers of defence; character-level checks cannot stop a forged fact.
  • Ch. 15 — Compliance and Data Sovereignty — Self-hosting transfers compliance responsibility rather than eliminating it; the deliverable is an evidence chain, not a feature list.

Part V · Optimization (Chapters 16–19) ​

  • Ch. 16 — Benchmarks: A Reproducible Methodology — Three trust tiers, five essentials, three red lines. This chapter also states explicitly that our own published scores have not yet been independently recomputed by a complete in-repository run.
  • Ch. 17 — Benchmark Controversy and Evaluation Traps — Five variables each capable of a double-digit gap; why the first selection question is "how many turns", not "what is your score"; five concrete traps.
  • Ch. 18 — Quality Measurement and the Badcase Loop — Six quality dimensions in structural conflict; honest metrics return null rather than 0; the value of a badcase loop is in attribution, not the fix.
  • Ch. 19 — Controlled Self-Evolution: From Storage to Experience — Storage → reflection → experience, each failing differently. Mandatory preconditions is the dividing line between an experience and a suggestion.

Part VI · Practice (Chapters 20–22) ​

  • Ch. 20 — Comparing the First Tier — Write paradigms have converged: single-pass ADD-only is now the industry floor and no longer a differentiator. Differentiation lies in conflict adjudication, deletion provability, audit locatability, and cost structure.
  • Ch. 21 — Selection Decisions and Migration Paths — Five prerequisite questions, a decision tree, and three migration paths whose cost tracks architectural position rather than feature gap.
  • Ch. 22 — Representative Scenarios in Practice — Five scenarios, each with one decisive failure mode that is usually not "remembered the wrong thing", and one scriptable acceptance metric.

Part VII · Outlook (Chapter 23) ​

  • Ch. 23 — Unsolved Problems and the 2027 Roadmap — Who defines factual correctness, cross-agent arbitration, the verifiability of forgetting, the benchmark gap, and the cost-quality frontier. The bottleneck in each is an incentive structure, not a technique.

Appendices ​

  • Appendix A — Glossary — 30 terms with unified renderings, definitions, and the confusable pairs.
  • Appendix B — References and Reproducible Resources — Papers with arXiv identifiers, official product documentation, and the exact queries anyone can recompute.

This book cites Mnemosyne OS extensively, not because it is superior, but because it satisfies two conditions at once: the design documentation matches the implementation source, and the key capabilities can be verified locally. You can pip install mnemosyne-os and reproduce the behaviours described in the book rather than taking the prose on trust.
